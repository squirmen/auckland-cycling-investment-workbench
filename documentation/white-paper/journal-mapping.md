# Publication venue mapping

Check each venue's current author guidance immediately before submission.
Journal scope, format, fees, and open-access policy can change.

## Primary recommendation: Journal of Transport and Land Use

- Fit: interdisciplinary transport/land-use planning, modelling, network
  science, and open decision-support; it published the original PCT paper.
- Manuscript emphasis: reproducible method, cumulative network effects,
  transferability, and honest treatment of spatial/geographic uncertainty.
- Evidence threshold: complete Auckland demonstration plus robust validation
  and sensitivity; avoid a software-description-only paper.
- Strength: direct intellectual lineage and open-access readership.
- Risk: the land-use interaction must be substantive enough; add accessibility
  and destination/geography interpretation rather than presenting only routing.
- Scope: <https://www.jtlu.org/index.php/jtlu/about>.

## Alternative: Journal of Transport & Health

- Fit: active travel, population health, inequalities, intervention and policy
  development, health/economic impact assessment, and methodological advances.
- Manuscript emphasis: valid incremental cycling and health pathway,
  distributional analysis, and transferability beyond Auckland.
- Evidence threshold: current MBCM-compatible health appraisal, no benefit
  double counting, strong sensitivity, and preferably empirical validation.
- Strength: PCT health-impact lineage and practitioner relevance.
- Risk: unsuitable if the paper remains primarily a network engineering or map
  tool; the journal asks for genuinely original, transferable health insight
  and discourages formula-heavy presentation.
- Scope: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-transport-and-health/about/aims-and-scope>.

## Alternative: Case Studies on Transport Policy

- Fit: applied policy case with practical transfer lessons.
- Manuscript emphasis: Auckland governance/problem framing, how reproducible
  evidence changes option development, programme dependencies, and a portable
  implementation protocol.
- Evidence threshold: decision-process insight and lessons for other regions,
  not only technical performance.
- Strength: accommodates a rich real-world case.
- Risk: novelty may appear local unless transfer conditions are explicit.
- Scope: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/case-studies-on-transport-policy/about/aims-and-scope>.

## Short-result route: Findings

- Fit: one concise, pointed, reproducible result—for example, the measured
  difference between static ranking and cumulative rerouting.
- Manuscript emphasis: a single question, minimal method needed to reproduce it,
  direct figure/table, and restrained interpretation.
- Evidence threshold: complete release and a clean empirical contrast.
- Strength: open, peer-reviewed, short format.
- Risk: cannot carry the entire system, evidence matrix, or Auckland planning
  narrative in one article.
- Scope: <https://findingspress.org/about>.

## Possible two-output strategy

1. Full method and Auckland application for the Journal of Transport and Land
   Use, with the decision brief released as a public companion.
2. A later Journal of Transport & Health paper only after local health,
   distributional, and prospective intervention evidence is strong enough; or
   a short Findings paper isolating the cumulative-routing result.

Do not submit substantially overlapping manuscripts without transparent
cross-reference and publisher permission.
