    What the University already has

    Ten card-access bike stores on the City campus, including the bike shed beside Hiwa; the OGGB store alone holds 84 bikes with 12 charging bays. Grafton has a shed under Building 505, and Newmarket has secure racks at Buildings 901, 906 and 907. Public docks such as Locky Docks add something different: visitor and short-stay parking on the public face of the campus, free charging, and a tap-in, no-card option. None exist on a UoA campus today. The nearest are outside Les Mills, across Khyber Pass Road from the Newmarket campus, and at AUT and the Central City Library, near the City campus.

    STAND: Secure Two-wheeler Access Network Design

    STAND is part of SPAN (Spending Priorities for Active Networks), the Better Places Lab's open tool for deciding where to invest in cycling. It is SPAN's first bike parking tool, built on SPAN's traffic-stress network: each street's Level of Traffic Stress comes from SPAN, and riders are routed with its costs for stress and gradient. A general bike parking tool for SPAN is planned.

    STAND answers one question for the University of Auckland's City, Grafton and Newmarket campuses: where should secure, free-to-use bike docks stand? It ranks every walkable spot on each campus, then chooses a set of docks that serves the most cycling arrivals, in visible, lit, flat places on the paths riders already use.

    How it works

    • Arrivals. Each University building draws cyclists in proportion to its floor area and use (teaching and libraries weigh more than research labs; halls of residence are origins, not destinations). Campus totals are scaled to typical-weekday cyclist arrivals (City 900, Grafton 140, Newmarket 90), derived from headcounts and assumed cycling shares informed by Auckland Transport's 2018 tertiary survey, and stated as assumptions.
    • Approach flows. Riders come from homes within 400 m of the campus study areas (residential floor area) and, mostly, from further out through ten corridor portals, eight weighted by AT counters and two by judgement. The eight are Quay St, Nelson St, K Rd, Upper Queen St, Symonds St, Carlton Gore Rd, Grafton Gully and Great South Rd; Broadway and Parnell Rd have no counter. Each rider takes the least-stress route: the Overture/OpenStreetMap network with a Level of Traffic Stress class per link and gradients from LINZ 1 m LiDAR. Riders keep to one-way rules; the walk from a dock to a building does not have to. Flows are accumulated on every path, so the model knows which side of a building riders arrive from.
    • Candidates and scores. Every walkable network node, path midpoint, existing rack and car-park inside the campus is a candidate. Each gets eight indicators: buildings within a short walk, riders passing, a calm approach, eyes on the street (footpath density, active frontage, open sightlines from the LiDAR surface model), lighting, room and power, flat ground, and open racks mapped nearby. Each is scored 0–1. Buildings within a short walk, riders passing and the footpath and frontage parts of eyes on the street are scaled within each campus; the rest use fixed formulas. The scores are then weighted (defaults 30/20/10/15/8/7/5/5).
    • Choosing the set. A mixed-integer programme picks k docks that maximise covered arrivals (each building credited once, to its best dock, full credit within 50 m walk falling to nothing at 250 m) plus a suitability bonus, at least 110 m apart. k is swept from 1 to 8 so the gains from each extra dock are visible; the docks are numbered so that each adds the most new coverage given the ones before it.

    Read it with care

    • Arrival totals scale the flows; site rankings barely move with them, but dock counts do. Treat dock numbers as indicative and grow them with observed utilisation, as at Glen Eden.
    • OpenStreetMap lamp and rack coverage is incomplete; lighting and open racks nearby are soft signals to confirm on site.
    • Traffic stress on roads and cycleways comes from SPAN's way-level classification where it covers the way (it carries Auckland Transport's facility data) and from a class-and-speed rule elsewhere; footways and paths count as low stress. Each link says which.
    • Findings from other secure bike parking pilots should be checked against these results.
    • Every recommended spot needs a field check: exact kerb or plaza position, the 4.8 × 2.2 m footprint, a power connection, CCTV and lighting, and who owns the ground.
