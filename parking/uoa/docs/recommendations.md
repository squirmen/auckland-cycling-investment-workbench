# Where secure bike docks should stand on the University of Auckland campuses

Better Places Lab, September 2026. STAND version 0.1.0.

STAND stands for Secure Two-wheeler Access Network Design. It is part of [SPAN](https://span.tfwelch.com/)
(Spending Priorities for Active Networks), the Better Places Lab's open tool for deciding where to invest
in cycling.

## The short answer

Twelve docks across the three campuses would put 89% of the City campus's estimated cyclist arrivals,
83% of Grafton's and almost all of Newmarket's (99.9%) within a short walk of a secure dock. Five of them
do most of the work and should go in first. Five more follow in a second stage, and two are optional.
Without the two optional docks, the shares are 85% at City and 76% at Grafton.

Coordinates are the modelled network points; the exact position (which kerb, which bay) is a site
decision within about 20 m. "Arrivals within reach" counts each building's modelled arrivals with full
credit within a 50 m walk, falling to none at 250 m. "Adds" is what a dock brings within reach that the
docks before it on the same campus do not. "Riders pass" is the modelled number of cyclists a day whose
route goes through the point. Traffic stress on the approaches comes from SPAN's way-level
classification for 9,782 of the 22,918 network links (the road and cycleway links SPAN covers, which
include the AT facility overrides) and from the class-and-speed rule elsewhere; footways and paths count
as LTS 1. Eight of the ten portal weights are based on AT
counters (two blend two counters); the Broadway and Parnell Rd weights are assumptions.

## Why Alfred Street is not on the list

Earlier runs of the model put the first City dock on Alfred Street, between Kate Edger and the General
Library. It is still the best single spot on the City campus: the model picks it when only one dock is
allowed, and it scores highest of any spot on the three campuses (0.88), with four mapped lamps. It is
not in the set of six because the Old Choral Hall lane, 92 m away, reaches more of the northern precinct
now that people can walk along one-way lanes (see the method, section 9). The Thomas Building, Commerce A,
Alfred Nathan House, Clock Tower East and the Old Arts Building are 26–82 m from the lane but 111–179 m
from Alfred Street. Docks must be at least 110 m apart, so the two cannot both be chosen. The six docks
above cover 799 arrivals a day; with Alfred Street in place of the lane they would cover 776. The old
optional dock at the Thomas Building drops out for the same reason, and the optional sixth dock is now
Waterloo Quadrant.

## How many docks

The model estimates cyclist arrivals per weekday at 900 (City), 140 (Grafton) and 90 (Newmarket),
from on-campus headcounts and assumed mode shares (AT's 2018 tertiary survey found 1% of UoA students
cycling; staff cycle more; e-bikes have grown since). Not all of them want a public dock: the City
campus already has ten card-access stores, and the OGGB store alone holds 84 bikes. A planning share of
10–15% of covered arrivals for public docks, plus NZTA's 10–25% headroom, gives roughly:

| Campus | Arrivals covered by the recommended set | Docks at 12% share × 1.2 | Practical start |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| City | 799 of 900 | about 115 | 10-dock stations at the three first-stage sites (30), grow with use |
| Grafton | 117 of 140 | about 17 | one 10-dock station at Park Avenue |
| Newmarket | 89.9 of 90 | about 13 | one 5–10 dock station at the core |

Glen Eden's experience argues for starting modest and modular: five docks ran at 40% utilisation
overall, all five were in use at once at the March 2024 peak, and stays were long (sessions longer than
five minutes: median 9.6 hours, half of them starting in the 07:00 hour). Count bikes parked at each
site for a week before installation and again after three months.

## What the map lets you do

[The live map](../) lets you:

- pick a campus and see the suitability surface (hexagons about 50 m across), the modelled approach flows or the
  traffic-stress network, with every candidate site and the recommended docks numbered in the order
  each adds the most;
- move the "Docks on this campus" slider to see what each extra dock buys (exact solutions for 1–8);
- change what matters (eight weights) and watch the ranking and the selection update;
- click any site for its case in plain English, its scores, the buildings it serves and a
  pre-installation checklist; click any building for its arrivals and its nearest dock;
- switch to the Aerial basemap: LINZ 2024-25 urban aerials, resampled to 0.4 m, cover each campus, so
  kerbs, parking bays and plazas can be seen.

## Things to confirm on site

For every first-stage site: the 4.8 × 2.2 m footprint and a route for the 1.5 kW supply (a 10-dock
charging station), CCTV coverage and night lighting (OpenStreetMap lamp data are patchy, so the model's
lighting score is a soft signal), sightlines from the busiest frontage, and whether the ground is
University land or road reserve. In OpenStreetMap, the three first-stage City sites and the Newmarket
core path lie inside the University's mapped campus, while Park Avenue lies just outside it, on the
street. A map is not a land title, so confirm who owns the ground; a dock on road reserve needs Auckland
Transport's approval. Also check what the Symonds St redesign in the City Centre Masterplan will change.
